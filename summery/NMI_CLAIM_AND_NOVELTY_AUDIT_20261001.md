# NMI claim and novelty audit — 2026-10-01

## Purpose and scope

This is a bounded project-position audit, not a systematic review and not an experimental result. It combines the current repository evidence with a targeted primary-literature check to prevent the next study from repeating an already established algorithmic claim. Literature search was limited to the most relevant source papers and is not exhaustive.

## What the biological sources already establish

**M2 / C. elegans corollary discharge.** Ji et al. report that motor-circuit feedback is represented in AIY, that RIM is required for this motor-state representation, and that RIM perturbation weakens sustained forward movement during thermotaxis. The biological result is a bounded sensory-circuit feedback and motor-state persistence finding; the project should not claim to rediscover it. See the [source article](https://elifesciences.org/articles/68848).

**M5 / cerebellar teaching events.** The project record links timed climbing-fiber teaching evidence to an eligibility-trace abstraction. The exact exponential trace equation and its transfer to artificial learning remain computational hypotheses rather than a directly measured circuit rule; see the [M5 evidence and claim-boundary record](M5_CEREBELLAR/RESULTS.md).

## What the artificial results currently support

M2's artificial results are conditional and comparator-sensitive. Scalar state-estimation experiments found benefits under some aligned mappings, but task-aware Kalman references remained stronger. The first 2D closed-loop run lost to capacity-matched recurrent controls. The post-result V2 diagnostic found that changing the shared training trajectory distribution shifted the bilinear-minus-MODE_GAIN final-distance contrast by `+0.02279` (crossed 95% interval `[+0.01341,+0.03318]`) and reversed its mean sign. Yet the additive control remained better. A newer, source-aligned feedback-placement abstraction exceeded an equal-parameter output-persistence yoke by `+6.80` accuracy points at hazard `0.05`, but did not beat the no-feedback model, generic RNN, or Bayes reference. The evidence therefore supports sensitivity to training distribution and a bounded stability/switching effect in synthetic controllers; it does not establish an overall AI benefit or transfer from the worm.

M5's simplified eligibility trace beats selected no-trace/local-update controls in several synthetic delayed-credit tasks, but exact replay, BPTT, or batch-learning references remain stronger in the project's tested conditions. The positive contrast is therefore a local algorithmic effect, not a general learning advantage. A new adjacent Dryad reward-interval analysis found the source-style CF-LTD projection worse than the training-fold session mean by `0.00970 s` on average (only 1/16 sessions improved); generic raw-GrC PCA16/ridge showed a small, group-dependent signal and beat CF-LTD in all three groups. This exploratory session-level result does not challenge the separate delay-eyeblink CF intervention evidence, and it does not establish a new biological or AI mechanism.

Fish1.5 supplies explicit same-specimen neuron-ID registration for listed crosswalk rows, but its inspected public release does not identify the frozen direction-switch/evidence-update computation. The registry records acquisition `PARTIAL`, with E3 closed; it cannot currently serve as a positive structure–function mechanism result.

## Novelty boundary from primary literature

Eligibility traces and online local temporal credit assignment are established computational ideas. Bellec et al.'s e-prop work already combines synaptic eligibility traces with learning signals to train recurrent spiking networks on temporal-credit tasks ([Nature Communications, 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-17236-y)). A recent hardware study implemented reward-modulated STDP with decaying eligibility traces for in-situ spiking reinforcement learning ([Nature Communications, 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-69898-9)). These precedents do not invalidate the project's bounded cerebellar comparison, but they make “eligibility traces enable delayed credit assignment” insufficient as the paper's novel central claim.

Likewise, “motor feedback sustains a neural state” is already the central result of the Ji et al. biological study. Reimplementing that statement in an artificial network, without a new computational prediction and discriminating controls, would be an application demonstration rather than a new mechanism discovery.

### Action-conditioned state updates are established AI prior art

Shaj et al. introduced Action-Conditional Recurrent Kalman Networks at CoRL 2020 (published in PMLR 155 in 2021). Their model explicitly adds an action-dependent control term to a latent-state transition, supports missing observations, and was evaluated against recurrent baselines and analytical models on robot forward/inverse dynamics. The paper therefore predates this project's artificial use of motor/action input in recurrent state updates. The authors' [PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v155/shaj21a.html) and [official implementation](https://github.com/ALRhub/action-conditional-rkn) are direct sources; the repository was inspected at commit `7a8ae18c17ff3f324f58a6d2ef8ee93265a6fa07` and carries an MIT license.

**Inference:** the new `M2_RECIPIENT_SPECIFIC_FEEDBACK_YOKE_V1` result establishes recipient-specific contingency only in its tested simulator. It does not establish a novel AI architecture or a new general principle of action-conditioned recurrence. Existing comparisons against small generic models are insufficient to make that broader claim.

**Revised experimental requirement:** any continued M2 artificial claim must be tested against an action-conditioned recurrent state-space baseline, not only an ordinary RNN, and must identify what the biological derivation predicts beyond inserting actions into latent dynamics. The next comparison should count parameters, training examples, optimizer updates, and measured inference compute; retain a task-aware oracle; test observation loss and action/dynamics mismatch; and preserve a recipient-identity yoke. A positive result would still be an artificial comparison, not new biological validation. This is a targeted comparative experiment, not another data-acquisition search.

## Assessment

**Fact:** the repository contains multiple M2 and M5 source-model and synthetic runs, including null/adverse results and strong task-aware or generic baselines. These results are documented separately and are not pooled.

**Inference:** current results do not yet support a single NMI-level positive claim spanning biological evidence and AI benefit. In particular, the artificial M2 reliability-gating task does not directly instantiate the experimentally supported RIM→AIY corollary-discharge computation; the M5 local trace abstraction is not novel by itself and remains below replay-based references.

**Recommendation and update:** a source-equation stress test found sensory-site feedback exceeded the no-feedback ablation in simulated local progress across stationary and reversing gradients, with reduced progress under faster reversals. However, moving the same coefficient into the motor-state equation saturated the output-only arm at 100% forward occupancy, invalidating that arm as a persistence-matched placement control. A subsequent cross-agent yoke preserved the source feedback signal's exact instantaneous population distribution while breaking recipient-specific contingency. Self-contingent feedback exceeded the yoke at 20-s reversals (+0.04618 aligned-progress units/s; 95% interval [+0.04252,+0.04994], 100/100 simulation blocks positive), but the small contrast reversed at 5 s. A new artificial tracking yoke found a larger recipient-contingency effect, but it reuses the prior tracking task family and was compared to a small one-state RNN; the task-aware oracle remained better. The later 2D pursuit comparison found mode-gain beat the adapted AcRKN in the aligned contrast, while an 8-parameter bilinear RNN beat it under both aligned and reversed mappings; the task-aware Kalman reference remained strongest. This weakens a distinctive M2 computation claim on that task, and the AcRKN arm was an adaptation rather than a full model reproduction. Together these support conditional effects in the implemented simulators, not a transferred AI principle. Next work should test a biologically anchored computation on an independently defined task against strong matched and task-aware controls, retaining recipient yokes and testing signal/task mismatch. Until then, report bounded source-model and simulator probes, not a novel AI principle.

**M5 follow-up:** a 64-value eligibility trace exceeded a one-vector exact FIFO by `+0.14779` accuracy in a post-result delayed-label comparison, but that FIFO skipped almost all updates at delays 4–64. Direct exact cue retention won at delay 1, and adequate-capacity replay outperformed the trace. This is a bounded memory/accuracy result on one synthetic task family. It does not resolve the novelty concern: eligibility traces and online temporal credit assignment have substantial prior art, and no shared M2/M5 artificial advantage is established.

The later delayed-reward bandit frontier used 30 disjoint task seeds. Trace exceeded current-score assignment by `+0.00948` expected reward, but its equal-state trace-minus-one-vector-FIFO contrast was `−0.00248` across delays, and exact FIFO/replay with enough state outperformed it. This adds a second synthetic task objective but remains a post-result comparison; it does not establish architecture or task generalization, an advantage over strong exact-memory methods, or biological transfer. It therefore does not materially change the publication-level novelty assessment.

### Latest M2 burst-duration transfer experiment

`M2_BURSTY_OBSERVATION_FEEDBACK_V1` varied the mean duration of Markov observation-loss bursts while holding expected missingness at 50%. At the frozen long-burst condition, sensory-site feedback beat the equal-parameter output-site control by `+0.05932` MSE (paired crossed 95% interval `[+0.05201,+0.06673]`; 24/24 seed means positive) and a four-parameter generic scalar RNN by `+0.06625` (`[+0.06000,+0.07283]`). Its difference from the 8-unit GRU was `+0.00756` (`[−0.00258,+0.01818]`), unresolved. Lower MSE coincided with lower action energy but slower post-gap recovery than output persistence. The contract's joint criterion against strong recurrence was not met. This narrows the synthetic boundary for feedback placement; it does not show that burst robustness is a property of the biological RIM–AIY circuit or establish an AI benefit. The experiment was informed by prior M2 outcomes. See the [full results and limitations](M2_BURSTY_OBSERVATION_FEEDBACK_V1/RESULTS.md).

### Latest M2 biological source-data reanalysis

`M2_AIY_STATE_CONDITIONAL_ENCODING_V1` reanalyzed constant-temperature traces from Ji et al. AIY-only decoding of forward/reversal state averaged AUC `0.758` in five WT animals and `0.502` in five RIM-ablated animals. After adding the immediately previous motor state as a strong persistence baseline, AIY's incremental AUC was small (`0.01554` WT; approximately zero after ablation). AVA showed a similar WT-to-ablation decrease in incremental AUC, so this does not isolate AIY-specific residual coding. The small public cohort and retrospective analysis make permutation and animal-bootstrap values descriptive. This source-data result is consistent with the published RIM-dependent representation but adds no new causal or AI evidence. It exposes a transfer-design constraint: an artificial test must compare neural feedback against explicit prior-state/action persistence, rather than treating decoder performance alone as circuit-added information. See the [contract and results](M2_AIY_STATE_CONDITIONAL_ENCODING_V1/RESULTS.md).

## Publication status

The work is active, but “NMI-ready” is not currently supported. A future paper needs a narrow central contribution, a source-grounded computational variable with a discriminating prediction, strong matched and task-aware controls, independent task generalization, complete provenance, and a manuscript-level novelty assessment. The latest M2 reanalysis sharpens the biological interpretation but does not improve the unresolved AI-transfer result. The new M5 source-data probe likewise narrows a modeled projection claim but supplies no positive artificial-transfer evidence. Further dataset acquisition is not the current bottleneck for the M2/M5 artificial studies; scientific alignment and comparative strength are.

### M2 AIY prospective-state source-trace follow-up — 2026-10-01

The post-result `M2_AIY_PREMOTOR_STATE_PREDICTION_V2` analysis predicted the next known movement state within 2 seconds from five recent motor-state samples, then added current AIY or AVA activity. AIY added mean animal-level AUC `0.2098` in WT and `0.0779` after RIM ablation; AVA added `0.3033` and `0.0804`, respectively. The larger AVA group contrast and sparse event counts (6–18 positive samples per animal) prevent an AIY-specific or robust group claim. V1's strict future-window definition had no scored positive RIM-ablated examples; V2 skips the source's transition/unknown samples and is explicitly an outcome-informed target-definition follow-up. This provides a candidate prospective neural signal for future tests but neither causal evidence nor an AI-transfer result. Independent score recomputation passed. See [V2 results](M2_AIY_PREMOTOR_STATE_PREDICTION_V2/RESULTS.md) and [V1 failure record](M2_AIY_PREMOTOR_STATE_PREDICTION_V1/FAILURE_LOG.md).

## 2026-10-01 M2 synthetic predictive-cue transfer boundary

The M2 transfer probe supplies an explicit negative boundary for the current abstraction. A six-parameter forecast-fusion filter benefited from an oracle-like synthetic future-state cue relative to its no-cue ablation, but a six-parameter generic recurrent model used the same cue better in the aligned primary condition (`AUC 0.8643` vs `0.7941`; matched contrast `−0.0702`, 95% seed-block interval `[−0.0724,−0.0681]`). The structured filter lost less under cue mismatch, but still fell below no-cue. Since the cue is generated from the future target and is not measured neural activity, this cannot establish biological-to-AI transfer. The project still has no supported cross-mechanism AI principle; this result argues against presenting the present M2 filter as a positive mechanism-shaped AI advantage. See the [root README](../README.md) and [`M2_PREMOTOR_SIGNAL_AI_TRANSFER_V2`](M2_PREMOTOR_SIGNAL_AI_TRANSFER_V2/RESULTS.md).

## 2026-10-01 M5 decay-horizon sweep: bounded gain, replay remains stronger

A 30-seed, four-delay sweep evaluated four fixed eligibility decays on the existing synthetic contextual bandit. Selected cells showed higher expected reward than applying each delayed reward to the current score, with familywise intervals excluding zero. However, exact replay remained substantially better (`0.55485` mean held-out expected reward versus `0.50660–0.51157` for traces), and every trace arm lost to replay in every delay cell. The result supports only a delay-dependent compact-state trade-off against a memory-poor baseline. Because the reward is caused by one exact past action, it does not establish that a temporally smeared eligibility state is the correct credit assignment, nor does it demonstrate cerebellar-to-AI transfer. The task generator, mechanism family, and earlier results were already known; classify this as exploratory. See [full results](M5_DELAYED_REWARD_TRACE_HORIZON_V1/RESULTS.md) and the [root README](../README.md).

## 2026-10-01 M2 transient-filter feedback-placement result — current evidence update

`M2_SENSORIMOTOR_TRANSIENT_FILTER_V1` tested a new synthetic controller in which actual motor state entered either a scalar sensory-state update or the output command. Under the frozen transient-pulse primary endpoint, output-site feedback outperformed sensory-site feedback: `OUTPUT_SITE_1D − SENSORY_SITE_1D = −0.0921` movement MAE (95% paired seed-block bootstrap interval `[−0.1081, −0.0766]`, 20 blocks). The no-feedback model also performed worse than both feedback models, while the GRU and fixed direct-sensor reference had different trade-offs across movement MAE, pulse-window MAE, and command energy. These are conditional simulator results; parameter equality does not equate computation, training cost, or optimization.

**Interpretation:** this result further weakens a broad claim that sensory-site placement has a distinctive artificial advantage. It is consistent with output-stage actuator correction being better suited to this task, but that explanation was not separately manipulated and remains an inference. The result neither refutes Ji et al.'s biological evidence nor establishes an alternate AI principle. The experiment was frozen within-study but post-result relative to the project, and remains exploratory.

**Current project consequence:** M2 remains a biologically supported source computation with several mixed or negative artificial translations. Do not promote its present artificial evidence to an NMI-level AI-benefit claim. The next mainline experiment should target a distinct, source-grounded computational prediction with a strong task-native control, or the paper should be repositioned around a precisely bounded transfer failure only if a broader, reproducible cross-mechanism pattern is established.
