# M5 delayed-reward trace-horizon sweep V1 — results

**Classification:** outcome-informed exploratory experiment. This tests one synthetic stationary contextual-bandit implementation and does not establish biological transfer or broad AI benefit.

## Result

Across 30 paired task seeds (`61–90`) and four reward delays (`D = 1, 4, 16, 64`), all four decaying-trace settings improved over immediate-score assignment in some delay cells. The effect varied with both decay and reward delay. The largest trace-minus-no-trace contrasts were `+0.03160` at `D=1, γ=0.75`, `+0.01902` at `D=4, γ=0.90`, `+0.00934` at `D=16, γ=0.98`, and `+0.00345` at `D=64, γ=0.98`. The first three contrasts and the `D=64, γ=0.98` contrast had Bonferroni familywise 95% intervals excluding zero; at `D=16/64`, shorter traces (`γ=0.50/0.75`) were unresolved against immediate-score assignment. These are cell-wise sweep outcomes, not independent confirmations or evidence for an optimally tuned gamma.

Exact replay was consistently stronger. Mean held-out expected reward averaged across delay cells was `0.55485` for exact replay, compared with `0.49932` for immediate-score assignment and `0.50660–0.51157` for the four trace settings. Every trace-versus-exact-replay paired interval was below zero in every delay cell (0/30 seeds positive in each comparison). Exact replay uses delay-sized storage (`32 × (D+1)` score values); a trace uses 32 eligibility values. Thus the trace offers compact state with a substantial accuracy cost in this task.

## Interpretation and limits

The decay sweep shows a delay-dependent compression trade-off against assigning a delayed reward to the current score. Longer traces tended to retain more useful history at longer delays, while short-delay tasks favored shorter traces. This does not demonstrate that the trace is the correct credit assignment: the one reward is generated by one past action, and exact replay preserves that action's score and substantially outperforms all traces. A compact trace blends multiple recent score vectors and can therefore assign reward to unrelated contexts.

This result narrows the practical claim to a low-state approximation that can improve over a deliberately memory-poor current-score update in a fixed linear-softmax bandit. It does not show superiority over exact causal replay, establish an empirically optimal decay constant, or test changing contingencies, nonlinear policies, recurrent-gradient controls, real sensory data, or cerebellar physiology. The four gamma values were fixed before this run; no value was selected from its test seeds. The experiment remains post-result exploratory because the bandit task family and previous trace results were already known.
