# Figure 1 | Sensory-site motor feedback improves tracking in some artificial regimes and incurs costs in others

The heat map shows mean paired tracking-error differences across nine target-switch-hazard × missing-sensation conditions. Each cell is `MSE(comparator) − MSE(SENSORY_SITE_FEEDBACK)`; positive values favor the sensory-site update and negative values favor the comparator. Teal indicates positive differences and terracotta indicates negative differences. The outlined column marks the training condition (`hazard=1/120`, 50% missing). At that condition, the generic one-state RNN minus sensory-site contrast was `+0.04386` MSE (crossed 95% bootstrap interval `[+0.04247,+0.04526]`); output-site persistence and no-feedback contrasts were `+0.00403` and `+0.00416`. The interval resamples 32 training-seed clusters and 256 shared held-out episode IDs (10,000 crossed-bootstrap resamples); each condition also contains a task-aware oracle with access to the true relative target error. Episodes are simulated observations, not biological replicates. The experiment uses an artificial telegraph target and exact-zero encoding for missing sensory samples. Results do not demonstrate a biological implementation or general AI benefit. Machine-readable source data: [`episode_results.csv`](../../data/results/M2_MOTOR_FEEDBACK_SPARSE_TRACKING_V1/canonical/episode_results.csv).
